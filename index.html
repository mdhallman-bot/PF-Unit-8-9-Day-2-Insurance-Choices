The central tension of insurance

You know what protection costs. You don't know what a year without it might cost.

You might pay premiums for twelve ordinary months and never file a claim. Or one accident or illness could create a financially catastrophic loss. Buying insurance does not guarantee a claim—and declining it does not eliminate the risk.

Ask yourself: Could Jordan pay for the worst credible loss without shifting the burden to someone else?

Insurance in plain language

Auto: Liability addresses others' injury/property losses. Collision addresses your own car after a collision. Comprehensive addresses noncollision losses.
Health: Helps with covered medical expenses, with premiums, deductibles, and coinsurance.
Renters/homeowners: Renters often protects belongings, personal liability and certain extra living expenses. Homeowners additionally generally protects the dwelling itself. Flood and earthquake often require different coverage.
Disability: Replaces part—not usually all—of qualifying lost earnings after a covered illness or injury. A waiting period can create a cash-flow gap.
Life: A policy pays a death benefit to a beneficiary. A parent naming a child as beneficiary does not mean the child's own life is insured.
Also exists: Long-term care coverage, identity theft expense coverage, and professional liability. Their purposes differ from the five categories investigated deeply today.
Part 2A · Auto insurance

When does car insurance actually pay?

Colorado minimum required liability insurance: 25/50/15. That means $25,000 bodily injury for one person, $50,000 bodily injury total per accident, and $15,000 property damage per accident. Minimum does not mean sufficient. Colorado DMV source.

“Full coverage” is casual shorthand, not a standardized policy that pays for everything. A financed car's lender commonly requires collision and comprehensive. For another-driver-at-fault crashes, the other driver's liability may pay, but disputed fault, insufficient limits, and uninsured drivers complicate recovery.

What changes auto premiums? What are license points?

Factors that can change a quote:

  • Age and driving experience: newer drivers commonly pay more than experienced drivers.
  • Driving record: tickets and crashes may raise premiums.
  • Location: hail, theft, congestion, and local repair costs may matter.
  • Vehicle and repair technology: newer safety cameras can make windshield repair more expensive.
  • Annual mileage: more driving can mean more exposure to risk.
  • Deductible and coverage limits: a higher deductible can lower the premium but increases claim-time costs.
  • Other permitted factors: every insurer weighs information somewhat differently.

License points aren't the same as insurer rating points. Colorado DMV points are connected to violations and possible license suspension, but insurers use their own pricing systems. Drivers ages 18–20 face point thresholds of 9 in 12 months, 12 in 24 months, or 14 before age 21; ages 21+ have thresholds including 12 in 12 months or 18 in 24 months. Colorado DMV points information.

Windshield choice

Illustrative repair estimates: older car $300; newer car with driver-assistance camera calibration $1,100. Comprehensive deductible $500 unless a separate glass benefit applies.

Colorado does not require every auto policy to provide zero-deductible glass replacement. Some glass endorsements or benefits change the result.

$50,000 damage to someone else's property

Assume Jordan is legally responsible and two possible liability limits would pay up to their limits.

Potential unpaid liability is not automatically a bank loan or immediately collectible balance.

Collision worked example: $5,000 covered own-car damage with a $1,000 collision deductible → driver pays $1,000; insurer pays $4,000, assuming no other limits. Liability insurance is different: it concerns harm owed to others and is limited by coverage limits.

After a crash: make a report and file a claim

  1. Check safety: Stop, check for injuries, and call 911 in an emergency.
  2. Contact law enforcement: In Colorado, qualifying crashes with injury or property damage must be reported. Ask whether an officer will respond and request a case or police report number.
  3. Document and exchange details: Gather names, insurance information, photos and witness contacts when safe.
  4. Notify your insurer: Explain what happened, provide the police report or case number when available, and follow the claims instructions.
  5. Review the result: Check the deductible, covered amount, and any denial or dispute.

A police report is useful and reporting an accident may be legally required, but an officer may not attend every minor crash and not every claim requires a police report. If officers do not attend, ask about an appropriate online/counter report. Colorado DMV crash reporting.

Part 2B · Health insurance

Monthly cost versus worst-case exposure

At Jordan's age, coverage might come through an employer, a parent's qualifying plan (generally until 26), a Marketplace plan, or Medicaid if eligible. Where coverage comes from is different from how the plan works. HMO/PPO describe network arrangements; Bronze/Silver/Gold describe broad cost-sharing levels. Why networks, prescriptions, and medical bills complicate this decision

An in-network provider usually has different cost sharing than an out-of-network provider. A formulary and prior authorization can matter. An EOB explains claim processing and is not itself a bill. Even insured people can face significant debt from deductibles, excluded care or other charges.

Without insurance, a hospital's sticker charge is not necessarily the final amount: patients can request itemized bills, ask about charity care or discounts, and discuss payment arrangements. Bankruptcy is a possible last-resort legal process in severe debt cases—not an automatic outcome and not always a complete solution. CFPB medical-debt information.

Part 2C · Other protection

Renters, life and disability

Renters and homeowners

Renters insurance commonly includes belongings, personal liability and certain additional living expenses. A landlord's property insurance generally does not insure the tenant's possessions. Homeowners also protects the dwelling. Flood and earthquake losses are often excluded by standard policies.

Example: A fire destroys $2,500 of Jordan's clothes and computer. Renters insurance may help after the deductible. If river flooding ruins the same possessions, a standard renters policy generally will not cover the flood unless a separate policy applies.

Actual cash value can be smaller than replacement cost. A rider/endorsement can modify protection for jewelry, sewer backup, or other specific risks.

Life insurance

For term life insurance, a named beneficiary receives the death benefit if the insured dies during the covered term. It is especially important to investigate when others financially rely on someone's earnings or care. A parent's life policy naming a child as beneficiary does not mean the child is insured under that policy.

Illustrative student quote: $250,000 term-life protection for $18/month. Actual eligibility and prices depend on the applicant and underwriting.

Example: A parent supporting children dies while term life coverage is in effect. A named beneficiary can receive the death benefit. If the parent becomes ill but survives, basic term life normally does not replace lost wages.

Disability income insurance

May replace a portion of earnings following a qualifying disability, often not the full salary. Consider the waiting period, definition of disability, duration and whether workplace benefits already exist.

Example: if pre-tax earnings are $4,500/month and a policy pays 60%, its hypothetical monthly benefit is $2,700 once eligible; the initial waiting period may pay $0. Tax treatment and offsets can change actual take-home benefits.

Example: Jordan cannot work for eight weeks after a covered injury. A policy with a 30-day waiting period might pay part of lost wages after that waiting period; a 90-day waiting period may pay nothing during those eight weeks.

Financial decision

Jordan has no dependents, relies on earnings, and rents. A cheap life policy isn't automatically the priority over protecting income. Conversely, some people who financially support children or others would weigh life coverage differently.

Part 4 · Jordan's Year

Twelve months. One plan. An uncertain future.

The months contain the same events for everyone. Your chosen coverage changes how the costs are shared. Most months are ordinary. Premiums continue in ordinary months—insurance was purchased before you knew which events would occur.

Ledger model: Every month begins with take-home income (except simulated work-loss months), subtracts essential expenses and premiums, then subtracts uncovered losses and claim-time cost sharing. Any remaining positive cash adds to savings. If cash is insufficient, a funding gap accumulates, not an automatic bank loan. Insurance claim payments reduce losses; they are not extra spendable income.
